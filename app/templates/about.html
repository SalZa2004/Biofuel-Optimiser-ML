About CNthesizer

An integrated computational platform for data-driven compression ignition fuel design.

Project Overview

CNthesizer was developed as part of a fourth-year integrated master group project at University College London. The project focuses on the use of machine learning, molecular data, optimisation, and generative design methods to support the discovery and evaluation of cleaner fuel candidates for compression ignition engines.

Compression ignition engines remain important in transport and industry due to their high efficiency, durability, and reliability under demanding conditions. However, continued reliance on conventional diesel fuels contributes to greenhouse gas emissions and particulate matter formation. This motivates the development of cleaner alternative fuels, biodiesel components, fuel blends, and additives.

CNthesizer brings together fuel property prediction, mixture analysis, biodiesel FAME-based cetane number prediction, screening constraints, and molecular generation into a single computational workflow.

Project Aim

The aim of this project is to support the efficient exploration of compression ignition fuel design space by developing a unified, data-driven framework that predicts key fuel properties, models mixtures and biodiesel formulations, performs screening and optimisation, and identifies promising fuel candidates and additives.

Platform Capabilities
Fuel Property Prediction

Predict key fuel properties such as cetane number, yield sooting index, boiling point, density, viscosity, and lower heating value from molecular structure.

Mixture and Blend Analysis

Estimate properties of multi-component fuel mixtures using component identities and composition fractions.

Biodiesel FAME Prediction

Predict biodiesel cetane number from fatty acid methyl ester composition, including the influence of chain length and degree of unsaturation.

Generative Fuel Design

Generate and screen new candidate molecules or additives using property prediction models, design constraints, and optimisation methods.
